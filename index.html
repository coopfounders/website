The problem

Barns are dense. Disease spreads before it's visible.

01

In large barns, respiratory issues can spread widely before birds show visible symptoms.

02

Early detection often depends on periodic walkthroughs and individual experience.

03

Air-quality stress amplifies respiratory problems, yet it isn't continuously monitored in many barns.

The solution

Continuous monitoring, not periodic checks.

Three signal classes, fused into one picture of barn health that runs around the clock.

Acoustic monitoring

A microphone array tracks cough and respiratory sound patterns continuously, against each barn's own baseline.

Air-quality sensing

Signals that amplify respiratory stress such as Ammonia, CO₂, temperature, and humidity are measured throughout the cycle.

Zone-level visibility

Distributed nodes localize where signals are shifting, so you know which part of the barn needs attention.

The technology

An edge-AI pipeline tuned for the realities of a barn.

Dusty, humid, and loud. The system processes locally, filters mechanical noise, and scores deviations against each barn's own baseline.

01

Sensor capture

Microphones record ambient audio; environmental sensors sample air conditions.

02

Noise filtering

Audio is filtered to suppress fans, feeders, and mechanical noise.

03

Feature extraction

Respiratory features and rolling air-quality metrics are computed.

04

Model inference

Current patterns are compared to baseline; deviations are scored.

05

Alert thresholds

Configurable thresholds trigger alerts with zone context.

06

Dashboard

Operators see trends, alerts, and zone-level breakdowns in one view.

Questions

The short version.

A few of the things growers and integrators ask us most.

We monitor sound plus air conditions because they interact. Ammonia and CO₂ can stress birds and amplify respiratory issues. Tracking both together gives a more complete picture than sound alone.
Climate controllers manage temperature and airflow. They do not detect biological risk. Coop monitors for respiratory patterns that suggest disease may be developing, alongside air-quality data that controllers often do not track at zone level.
The system is designed to operate locally with periodic sync. Data is processed on a local unit inside the barn and synced to the dashboard when connectivity is available. It buffers data during connectivity gaps.
Early versions focus on detecting elevated respiratory-risk signals, not diagnosing specific diseases. As we collect more labeled data from pilots, our ability to differentiate patterns will improve.
You do. Customer owns their data. We ask permission to use aggregated and anonymized data to improve our models, and you can revoke that permission at any time.
Yes. We are open to pilots with individual growers and with integrators. Enterprise rollouts across multiple barns are possible once the system is validated.
